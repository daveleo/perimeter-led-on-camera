Reading & recommendations

    How this simulator works

    Simplified engineering model. This tool models the principle of PWM + scan + camera interaction. It uses idealised rectangular pulses, one representative row group, a linear transfer unless gamma is set, and a schematic S-PWM / BCM distribution. Real driver ICs use proprietary pulse scheduling, ghost-cancellation, low-grey algorithms and calibration that this cannot reproduce. Use it to build intuition and to narrow down camera settings — confirm on the real system with a photodiode and oscilloscope.

    The layers it simulates

    • Content & frame rate — unique images per second and the output frame rate they are shown at (with pulldown cadence).
    • PWM scheme — conventional single-pulse PWM, S-PWM (sub-periods per frame), scrambled S-PWM, or binary-code modulation (BCM), optionally with a PWM→PAM low-grey hybrid.
    • Grayscale refresh — the visual repeat rate; for S-PWM this sets the number of sub-periods per frame.
    • Driver limits — bit depth, GCLK frequency and minimum pulse width, which together decide whether the requested refresh is even achievable and where low greys start to break.
    • Scan ratio — how many row groups share the driver outputs; sets each LED's maximum wall-clock ON fraction and the row-scan frequency.
    • Dimming method — global brightness split between PWM duty and current amplitude.
    • Camera — frame rate, shutter (time or cine angle), rolling vs global shutter, sensor readout time, genlock and exposure phase.

    The outputs

    • Simulated camera capture — a synthetic frame showing the bands, brightness steps and colour shifts a sensor would record with the current settings.
    • Optical waveform — emitted light vs time with the exposure window overlaid.
    • Flicker spectrum — the frequency content of the light, with the frame, scan and PWM components marked, and the camera shutter's frequency response (a sinc) overlaid: where a display component lands on a null it is suppressed; between nulls it passes through as flicker.
    • Phase response — captured brightness as the exposure start phase moves through one beat period; the peak-to-peak swing is the frame-to-frame flicker amplitude on camera.
    • Shutter sweep — that swing measured across the whole shutter range, so the "clean windows" are visible.
    • Filmstrip — consecutive captured frames, so beat and rolling-band drift are visible over time.

    Metrics

    • Percent flicker and flicker index of the optical waveform, with the IEEE 1789-style direct-vision risk classification (almost always "no effect" at these frequencies — the point is the camera metrics below).
    • On-camera flicker amplitude — peak-to-peak captured-brightness swing vs phase, as % of mean.
    • Band contrast — Michelson contrast between the brightest and darkest rows of the simulated rolling-shutter frame.
    • Capture ratio — captured light vs a long-exposure reference (100% = faithful).
